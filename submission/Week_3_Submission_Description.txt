Week 3 Submission: Predictive Analytics and Risk Modeling for Construction Projects

This report presents a Python-based predictive analytics workflow designed to demonstrate how construction project teams could identify early warning signals for schedule delays and cost overruns. The report begins with a construction risk register covering schedule and weather disruption, supplier and procurement problems, labor availability, ground/site uncertainty, design changes, rework, budget pressure, and safety incidents. It describes how these risks can affect delivery, cost, and operational control, and maps them to indicators that project managers can monitor.

For this practical exercise, a reproducible dataset of 500 simulated construction projects was generated with Python. The dataset includes project type and scale, contract value, planned duration, complexity, rainfall days, supplier delay days, workforce availability, design changes, ground-risk score, safety-training score, schedule buffer, subcontractor rating, and inspection/rework events. The report clearly labels the records and outcomes as synthetic rather than real project history.

The analysis compares Linear Regression and Random Forest Regression for estimating schedule-delay days, and Logistic Regression and Random Forest Classification for identifying projects with a simulated cost overrun of at least 10%. An 80/20 train-test split is used, with five-fold cross-validation on training records. Regression performance is assessed through MAE, RMSE, and R-squared. Classification is evaluated using ROC-AUC, precision, recall, F1, and accuracy. Preprocessing is performed inside scikit-learn pipelines to reduce data leakage, and the report discusses why classification thresholds should reflect operational priorities rather than accuracy alone.

The deliverables include diagnostic visualizations, a holdout confusion matrix, model metrics and coefficients, project-level holdout predictions, a mitigation register, and a scenario analysis comparing a representative high-risk project under baseline, supplier coordination, design-freeze, workforce, and combined-control assumptions. In the selected simulated case, combined controls lower predicted delay from 73.30 to 57.97 days, but the report cautions that this is an illustrative model output and not proof of causal impact.

Finally, the report proposes practical mitigations, governance safeguards, data-leakage checks, and monitoring steps for a potential real-world pilot. Actual historical project data, stakeholder-approved thresholds, time-aware validation, and human review would be required before operational use. The attached GitHub repository contains the data generator, modeling script, reproducible outputs, report, plots, and README.
