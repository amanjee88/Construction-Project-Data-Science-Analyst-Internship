This report presents the final synthesis of the four-week Construction Project Data Science Analyst internship project, with a focus on data visualization, stakeholder reporting, and actionable insights. It connects the Week 1 data acquisition and quality-preparation strategy with the Week 2 exploratory data analysis and the Week 3 predictive analytics and risk-modeling work. The report is organized for construction project stakeholders, including project managers, project controls teams, procurement leads, cost controllers, site managers, and Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) teams.

The analysis communicates key performance indicators from the Week 2 simulated dataset, including average schedule delay, the on-time completion indicator rate, average cost overrun, and procurement-delay patterns. Visualizations include an executive KPI dashboard, average delay by project type, procurement delay versus schedule delay, planned budget versus actual final cost, predictive-model validation trade-offs, model-based mitigation scenarios, and a qualitative risk-priority matrix. The accompanying narrative explains the meaning and limitations of each chart rather than presenting visual output without interpretation.

The report also summarizes the Week 3 model comparisons. Linear Regression and Random Forest Regression are reviewed for predicting schedule-delay days, while Logistic Regression and Random Forest Classification are compared for screening projects with a simulated cost overrun of at least 10%. The report discusses holdout metrics, five-fold cross-validation, the trade-off between precision and recall, and how such model outputs could support early review if they were validated on approved real-project history. A what-if scenario table illustrates how hypothetical supplier coordination, design-freeze, workforce, and combined controls change predictions for one simulated high-risk project.

Practical recommendations include weekly monitoring of long-lead procurement, critical-path constraints, open design changes and RFIs, labor availability, cost commitments, forecast final cost, rework, and safety actions. A 30-60-90 day implementation roadmap assigns suggested owners and measures of progress. Python tools such as pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and scikit-learn are explained in non-technical language so that stakeholders can understand how the analysis is produced and used.

All datasets and project outcomes used in this portfolio are simulated; Week 1, Week 2, and Week 3 are separate samples with different structures and are not combined as if they represented the same projects. Correlations do not establish causation, scenario changes are not guaranteed savings, and model metrics do not prove real-world predictive validity. The final recommendations therefore emphasize data quality, validation on real held-out projects, human review, transparent ownership, and safety controls that are never relaxed because of a model score.
