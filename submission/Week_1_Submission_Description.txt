This report presents a structured data acquisition and preparation strategy for a fictional construction project analytics scenario. The proposed analysis is intended to support budget monitoring, schedule visibility, resource tracking and early risk identification across a portfolio of building and civil-infrastructure projects. It defines the project context, objectives, intended decisions, scope, assumptions and candidate performance indicators before any predictive modeling is attempted.

The report compares several candidate information sources and explains what each can and cannot contribute. These include India’s Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation Infrastructure & Project Monitoring Division for high-level time/cost-overrun context, the Central Public Works Department’s Delhi Schedule of Rates as a dated rate-reference source, the World Bank’s public procurement-notice dataset for tender metadata, and India’s Open Government Data Platform for further infrastructure-resource searches. It also explains why detailed fields such as labor counts, equipment hours, change orders and site-level progress may require authorized project-control records or clearly labeled simulated data.

A proposed data dictionary covers project identifiers, type and location, baseline dates and budget, cost-to-date, planned and elapsed duration, physical completion, contractor experience, labor and equipment measures, material-cost context, procurement/weather delay, safety incidents, project status, source identifier and snapshot date. The preparation plan addresses missing values, duplicate keys, inconsistent categories, mixed numeric formats, invalid dates, unit conversions, outliers, baseline versioning and potential data leakage. It emphasizes preserving raw data, documenting transformations and routing ambiguous records to manual review rather than silently deleting or fabricating values.

The accompanying project package includes a small synthetic training CSV, a runnable Python profiling script, quality-profile and review outputs, a README and dependency file. The synthetic sample intentionally includes data-quality issues so the workflow can be demonstrated. It is not real project evidence and must not be used to make claims about actual construction performance. The report also provides an illustrative 32-hour work plan, governance controls, a risk register, preliminary hypotheses for later EDA and modeling, acceptance criteria and references to official source/documentation pages. The deliverable is designed to establish a traceable foundation for the next construction analytics tasks.
